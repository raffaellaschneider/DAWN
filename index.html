The project

From the cosmic dark ages to the first galaxies

The first stars and black holes transformed the early Universe, setting the stage for the formation and evolution of galaxies.

DAWN investigates how the first stellar populations and black-hole seeds formed, evolved and interacted with their environments. Combining advanced simulations, theoretical models and observations — particularly from the James Webb Space Telescope — the project aims to build a coherent picture of cosmic dawn in electromagnetic and gravitational-wave signals.

Principal Investigator: Prof. Raffaella Schneider · Department of Physics, Sapienza University of Rome · Funded November 2025 · Official project start March 2026.

Scientific objectives

Four questions. One cosmic story.

01 / STELLAR POPULATIONS

The first stars

Establish the nature of the earliest stellar populations, including their initial mass function, multiplicity and feedback on their environments.

02 / BLACK HOLES

Black-hole seeds and growth

Determine how the first seeds formed and which accretion and dynamical pathways enabled their subsequent growth.

03 / GALAXIES

The first galaxies

Clarify the processes regulating early galaxy evolution, including star formation, chemical enrichment, dust and feedback.

04 / MULTI-MESSENGER

Light and gravitational waves

Build a consistent view of cosmic dawn connecting electromagnetic observables and gravitational-wave predictions.

Research approach

Connecting physics across cosmic scales

DAWN combines hydrodynamical and N-body simulations with semi-analytic modelling.

The project links the sub-parsec physics of black-hole environments to the cosmological assembly of galaxies. Simulation-guided models, stellar population synthesis and the Cosmic Archaeology Tool (CAT) connect theoretical predictions to JWST and future gravitational-wave observations.

DAWN Kick-off Meeting

Ventotene, Italy · 7–9 September 2026

DAWN launched its shared scientific programme with an international meeting dedicated to the first stars, black-hole seeds and growth, the physics of early galaxies, and the observational signatures of cosmic dawn.

The meeting provided an opportunity to review ongoing activities, discuss the development of new tools and identify priorities for the next stages of the project.
